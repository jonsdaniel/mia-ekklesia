Editorial standards

Method & Sources

The site is an argument from the historical record. It does not ask the reader to accept a conclusion before seeing the evidence that bears it.

The central question

Can the same Church be identified at every point in Christian history?

“If Christ truly was born of the Virgin Mary, crucified under Pontius Pilate, suffered death and was buried, but on the third day rose again … then the Church which he established and built must still exist today.”

This line from the author’s testimony governs the chronology. A later claim is not admitted merely because it is Catholic; it must be compared with Scripture, early practice, patristic testimony, controversy, and authoritative definition.

Source hierarchy

  1. 01
    Scripture

    Read in its literary, historical, and ecclesial setting.

  2. 02
    Primary historical texts

    Fathers, liturgies, letters, canons, creeds, and eyewitness accounts.

  3. 03
    Councils and definitions

    Distinguishing local, ecumenical, disciplinary, and dogmatic authority.

  4. 04
    Secondary scholarship

    Used for dating, provenance, translation, scientific context, and competing interpretations.

Rules of argument

Sources first

The authoritative text is visually separated from commentary so a reader can inspect the evidence directly.

Context matters

A sentence is not allowed to prove more than its date, purpose, audience, and surrounding dispute can bear.

Development is identified

The site distinguishes first witness, ordinary practice, controversy, definition, and later theological explanation.

Objections stay visible

Serious alternative readings are stated with enough force that the reader can understand why the dispute remains.

Confidence is graded

A contemporary record, a later memory, and a modern inference are not presented as though they carry equal weight.

Credit is explicit

Where an article relies on another researcher’s work, the page names and links that author prominently.

Why this project exists

From the author’s testimony

“Either all of the world religions are false, and thus atheism is the natural consequence, or only one of them is true.”

That same logic later returned as an ecclesial question: either Christ’s Church disappeared, or it remains possible to locate her in history. The purpose of this site is to make that investigation readable, orderly, and open to inspection.
